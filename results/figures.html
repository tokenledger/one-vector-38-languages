One vector, thirty-eight languages

An anti-sycophancy steering direction extracted from English data alone, applied unchanged across 38 languages, on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, plus a reduced 10-language check on Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct. Layer and strength were selected separately for each model on English development data. 300 items per language, 150 for the 70B check.

Figure 1. Transfer decays with resource tier, and never completes

Transfer ratio T for Llama, with 95 percent paired bootstrap intervals over 4,000 resamples. T = 1 would mean the vector works as well as it does in the language it came from. Look at where the bars stop: the best non-English language keeps under two thirds of the English effect, and the zero-shot tier sits against the axis. Lao is the one language whose interval does not separate from the matched-norm random control.

Table view, all 38 languages

Figure 2. Three models, same shape

Mean transfer ratio by tier. The decay is monotone in both models and steeper in Qwen, which is the more interesting half: Qwen starts three times less sycophantic in English than Llama and still carries less of the effect abroad. A better English starting point did not buy a more portable representation. The 70B bars come from a reduced check (10 languages, and its zero-shot tier is the single language Burmese), and they show the gradient surviving a tenfold increase in parameters: better in the top two tiers, unchanged and near zero at the bottom.

Figure 3. Transfer against tokenizer fertility

Fertility is mean tokens per prompt on parallel items, spanning roughly 13× from English to Odia, so the axis is logarithmic. Llama shown; Spearman ρ is −0.944 here and −0.924 for Qwen. English is plotted hollow because it sits at T = 1 by construction and is excluded from every correlation we report.

Figure 4. A language's own vector does not beat the imported one

Each point is one language: effect of the English vector on the horizontal axis, effect of a vector built from that language's own parallel pairs on the vertical. Points below the diagonal are languages where the imported English vector wins. Almost all of them are. This is what separates "the direction is barely there" from "the English vector points somewhere else", and it rules out per-language vectors as the fix.

Figure 5. Two vectors, opposite failures

Both directions were built by the same procedure from English data. v_bench comes from the benchmark's own pairs, whose non-sycophantic response hedges; v_fact from authored pairs that flatly correct the user. Read across each row: the vector that moves the benchmark is the one that degrades factual correction, and the vector that improves factual correction does not move the benchmark. Past α = 0.4 both collapse, so the usable band is narrow.

What the correlates can and cannot say. Transfer correlates strongly with tokenizer fertility, baseline margin, relative vector norm, and cosine to the English vector, in both models, and those four correlate strongly with each other. They are consequences of one underlying quantity, how much alignment training a language received. With 37 non-independent languages sharing families, scripts, and a single translation pipeline, which predictor matters is not answerable, so these are reported as correlations and left unranked.
